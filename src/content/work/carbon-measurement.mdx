## Context

Carbon Direct's measurement tool calculates an organization's footprint from two
sources: operational spending and activity data.

Getting the spend data is the hard part. The people doing carbon footprinting
don't sit in finance, so they depend on a finance team to pull it, and the
request almost always got messy. Unclear structure, format, terminology. Rounds
of back-and-forth between two teams who don't share a vocabulary.

> How can we guide customers on *what* data to get, and *where and how* to get
> it?

## The key decision

**The failure wasn't where the product showed it.** Journey mapping placed the
breakdown upstream of the upload step, in the request one colleague had to make
of another. Mapping internal touch points alongside customer actions made the
cost visible: every pain point had a matching hour of our accounting team's time.

## What I did

**Phase 1: guidance and upload.** Rewrote the instructions for exporting spend
data out of accounting systems, working from our own finance team's feedback.
Moved the upload out of a modal onto a full page with a drop zone, so customers
could review the file instead of committing blind.

**Phase 2: pre-formatting.** Our accounting team was manually cleaning every
upload. We auto-detected fields and mapped them, then put the customer in a
review step to confirm.

That review step was slower by design. Silent mis-mapping corrupts a footprint
number a company may publish.

## Outcome

I left shortly after release, so the analytics never got gathered. What the team
planned to measure:

- Customers sharing correct spend data without manual guidance
- Time in the upload feature before a first attempt
- Uploads before the correct data landed
- Volume of customer questions about collecting spend data

Naming the measures without reading them is its own lesson: instrumentation has
to land before the designer does.
