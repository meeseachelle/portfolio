## Context

Kojo is a construction materials inventory platform. I led the workflow design
for vendors responding to requests for quotes, from onboarding through document
processing to review.

Vendors not yet integrated traded emails with customer success, then manually
entered every line item. Line items run into the hundreds. The quotes run from
hundreds of thousands to millions. A tedious step sat directly in front of the
most consequential transaction these businesses make.

## The key decision

**Start from recorded behavior, not the wireframes.** I tested the existing
designs internally and watched Fullstory recordings to find where vendors
actually stalled. The redesign responded to real friction rather than assumed
friction.

## What I did

**Three iterations, one page to stepper.** The single-page version tested worse
for a specific reason: on a quote with 300 line items, showing every stage at
once meant vendors never knew whether they were done. Splitting it gave the work
a visible end.

**Kept the source PDF beside the extracted table.** Vendors verify a scan
without switching context, the step that decides whether they trust it.

**Kept manual entry first-class**, not a fallback.

<Figure
  src={finalChoice}
  alt="Final entry screen offering two paths, upload quote PDF or manual entry, presented as two equally weighted cards"

**Tested with four participants:** two internal, two external vendors. They
understood the upload CTA and liked the dedicated pages. Fixes that came out of
it: limit uploads to one file, rename the confusing "next" buttons, make
scrollability obvious, consider bulk editing.

## Outcome

Release week:

- **701** unique users on the new bid portal
- **459** PDFs scanned
- **333** quotes still entered manually

> "You just saved me half the time it takes to enter a quote"
>
> Tom, Commercial Pipe & Supply

The 333 is the honest number. A third of vendors bypassed scanning. That gap
was next on my list.
