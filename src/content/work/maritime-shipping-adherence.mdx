## Context

Sofar's Wayfinder platform evaluates millions of routes a day and tells captains
when a better path to port opens up. It also sends a daily RPM recommendation.

Captains don't always follow it, and they're often right not to. Conditions
shift, and what's happening at sea doesn't appear in a forecast model. But every
deviation lowered the vessel's adherence score, leaving shoreside operators with
a number that said something went wrong without saying what.

> How can we allow shoreside operators to reasonably justify when captains do
> not adhere to our recommendations?

## The key decision

Justification, not compliance.

The obvious response to a low score is to push harder on adherence. That treats
the captain's judgment as the defect, when the captain has the most information
about the actual sea state. Everything else followed from refusing that framing.

## What I did

**Built the vocabulary from customers.** I asked what situations most often
required departing from guidance. Those answers became the reason taxonomy.

**Put the reasoning next to the data.** A column of notifications runs alongside
the adherence graph. Selecting one highlights the matching day on the chart.

A separate tab would have been cleaner to build. Side by side forces operators
to read cause and effect together, which is the whole point.

them into a status would have defeated the purpose.
